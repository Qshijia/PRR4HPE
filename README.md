# PRR4HPE

Exploring Penumbra Relationship Representation for Robust Head Pose Estimation under Extreme Rotation Scenarios

## :book: Abstract
Accurate head pose estimation constitutes a fundamental challenge in computer vision with significant implications for behavioral analysis and scene understanding. This task remains particularly challenging under unconstrained imaging conditions where illumination variation, extreme pose, and motion blur are prevalent. Severe self-occlusion and pronounced appearance variations under such conditions further complicate reliable estimation. Through systematic analysis of head pose images, we identify two crucial findings: local facial features exhibit systematic shape variations as the head rotates, while head profiles provide robust geometric cues, particularly in side views where texture is degraded. Guided by these insights, we propose a novel penumbra relationship network for head pose estimation (PRR4HPE), which introduces dual parallel pathways, visual tokens and penumbra tokens, with their contributions dynamically adjusted according to pose angle. This design effectively leverages structural profile information to achieve robust performance under extreme rotation. Specifically, we design a penumbra relationship mining (PRM) module to model high-level relational patterns among structural profiles, which explicitly formulates penumbra relationship as a structured geometric representation. We further develop a perceptive feature generation (PFG) module and a penumbra structure modeling (PSM) module to holistically integrate multi-source cues, incorporating both semantic and geometric information from head profiles. Extensive experiments on standard benchmarks including 300W-LP, AFLW2000, and BIWI validate that PRR4HPE achieves exceptional robustness in challenging environments and consistently exceeds state-of-the-art methods.

## :rocket: Overview
This section presents a detailed description of the proposed PRR4HPE architecture, with its overall framework shown in Fig. 4. The model comprises three fundamental components working in concert: PFG module employs an attention mechanism to dynamically enhance the representations of key profile regions. In this study, a novel encoding scheme ESF is proposed, which can be summarized into: embedding, scoring, and flattening; PSM module applies penumbra-aware representation learning to extract structural features from head profiles, with key steps comprising penumbra-aware profile extraction and penumbra token construction; PRM module utilizes a Transformer architecture to capture high-level semantic and contextual relationships among profile features, implemented through Transformer-based encoding and a prediction head. Finally, the model is optimized with a standard MSE loss combined with L2 regularization to prevent overfitting. Motivated by the two key findings, our method is designed with dual parallel pathways that explicitly capture both appearance-based and geometry-based cues. In frontal views, the U-shaped jawline contour serves as a stable symmetric reference; in side views, the complete profile arc spanning the forehead, eye-sockets, nasal-wings, mouth, and jawline provides rich structural information. The two pathways, visual tokens and penumbra tokens, are dynamically weighted according to pose angle. The cross-modal attention mechanism assigns adaptive weights to visual and penumbra tokens based on the estimated pose angle.

## :sparkles: Contributions
Based on these findings, this paper proposes a novel penumbra relationship network for HPE that leverages structural profile information to achieve robust performance under extreme rotation. The core idea is that dual parallel pathways, visual token and penumbra token, operate jointly with their contributions dynamically adjusted according to pose angle. The framework consists of three core components: 1) Perceptive feature generation module (PFG), where an attention mechanism is employed to dynamically enhance representations of key profile regions; 2) Penumbra structure modeling module (PSM), in which penumbra-aware representation learning is applied to extract structural features from head profiles; and 3) Penumbra relationship mining module (PRM), which captures high-level semantic and contextual relationships among profile features. Overall, the key contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
●	A novel penumbra relationship network for HPE (PRR4HPE) is proposed, which introduces dual parallel pathways, visual tokens and penumbra tokens, with their contributions dynamically adjusted according to pose angle, effectively leveraging structural profile information to achieve robust performance under extreme rotation.
●	PRM module is designed to model high-level relational patterns among structural profiles. This module explicitly formulates penumbra relationship as a structured geometric representation and integrates it with spatial geometric modeling for HPE.
●	A novel token-based representation learning approach is further introduced to acquire additional cues through structural profile modeling. The PFG and PSM modules effectively integrate multi-source cues, incorporating both semantic and geometric information from head profiles.
●	Comprehensive evaluation across three benchmark datasets demonstrates that PRR4HPE achieves state-of-the-art performance in both accuracy and robustness, representing a significant advancement in HPE. 

## :file_cabinet: Datasets

Three open-access datasets, BIWI, 300W_LP and AFLW2000, were utilized for model training and evaluation to test its adaptability across varied data scenarios. BIWI [32] contains 24 video sequences with 20 participants, including four individuals recorded twice, resulting in a total of 15,678 frames. The head regions in this dataset are relatively small within each image. This dataset includes challenging cases with large-angle head rotations. 300W-LP [33] employs sophisticated facial synthesis techniques to generate approximately 61,225 samples containing extreme head poses. The dataset is further expanded through mirroring, producing a total of 122,450 synthetic images. The synthetic samples cover a wide range of large-angle rotations, providing comprehensive coverage of extreme poses. AFLW2000 [34] comprises 2000 annotated images, each equipped with 3D facial ground truth annotations and 68 facial key-points. It is widely recognized as a challenging benchmark due to its inclusion of extreme head poses, diverse facial expressions, and varying illumination conditions.
Two standard experimental protocols were adopted for training and evaluation. Under Protocol I, the model is trained on the synthetic 300W-LP dataset and tested on the real-world AFLW2000 and BIWI datasets. Under Protocol II, the BIWI dataset is used for both training and evaluation, split into 70% for training and 30% for testing to ensure a fair comparison. This results in 10,975 frames for training and 4,703 frames for testing.
